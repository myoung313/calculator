Last-Five Prop Lab

Pull a player's recent games, set the sportsbook line, and see how often they clear it, how steady they are, and whether the price gives you an edge. Recent form gets blended with the season average so a hot week doesn't fool you.

How the numbers are calculated
Hit rate
How many of the last N games went over the line. Five games is a small sample: 4 of 5 happens by chance about 19% of the time even for a true 50/50 bet.
Projection
Recent average blended with the season average, weighting the season like 10 extra games. Streaks tend to fade back toward a player's normal level, and this captures that.
Over probability
Normal curve around the projection using the season's game-to-game spread. Low-count stats (home runs, touchdowns, goals) use a Poisson model instead. Whole-number lines exclude the push.
Edge and EV
Odds are turned into implied probability with the bookmaker's margin (vig) removed when you give both sides. Edge is model probability minus the fair price. EV is expected profit per $100 staked.
Consistency
Standard deviation divided by the average. Under 25% is steady; over 45% is boom-or-bust.
Minutes / volume
For basketball, a change in minutes often explains a streak better than talent does. A role change makes the season average less useful.

These are estimates from box scores. They don't know about injuries, matchups, rest, or lineup news, and sportsbooks already price recent form. Treat a flagged edge as a reason to look closer, never as a sure thing, and only bet what you can afford to lose.
